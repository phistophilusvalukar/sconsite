Backstory: Eiru was a land of beauty and prosperity. The Tuatha De Dannan weren’t the first people to invade this paradise, but it was their will that they would be the shepherds of the land, and ensure that the land wasn’t exploited and destroyed. Love made them decide to keep the land. Fear made them take action to defend it. The Fir Bolg were all but indigenous to the land, but their resistance had been brief, tempered when they saw that the Tuatha De were able to build and grow in harmony with the land. It was the Fomorians that made the Tuatha De begin to take drastic measures, towering brutes who took advantage of all kindness, and saw everything as theirs. An architect who was known for pulling the very metal for her spires from ore without disturbing stone, Brigh, was tasked to forge an undying force against the titanic Fomorians. Muin does not remember much of her life from before she was tasked to take on her metallic shell. She remembered being a slight, weak chinned, cowardly man, focused more on clock making and drawing than defending Eiru. She had been a student under Brigh, not the best or the brightest, but far from the worst of her apprentices. Her skill with working with machines had made her a clear candidate to aid the war effort as a mechanic, but her weak body was the issue, should she see combat. She doesn’t remember if she resisted or resented, but based on her later path, she reasons that she likely was happy to get out of that miserable pile of secrets, and become someone admirable. She had joined Brigh’s weapon against the Fomorians, a group of living weapons able to fight and die alongside their Shide brethren, only to be rebuilt so long as their nigh indestructible core could be recovered. The Mechanical Shide Unbroken, MSU, Muin Sil Ur phonetically, were among the many weapons brought to bear against the raiding giants, and helped to rebuff them from the paradise.

Their names were taken from them as a way to ease the passing, along with many of their personal memories. All that was supposed to remain was their skill and their compassion for their fellows. Not a person. Just a number, and a mission. Eventually, the Fomorians were defeated, and peace subsequently settled into Eiru. All of the MSU units were put into storage, set aside, should the land need to go to war again. But the Tuatha De Dannin were innovators, and built contingency upon contingency. By the time the Milasian Humans invaded, they were buried under centuries of other plans B through R, and were left untouched by the time the Humans were victorious, and the land was split between the Tuatha De and the Milasians, with the humans on the Material, and the Shide in the First World. For centuries, row upon row of automatons stood at attention, the kelly green of their lights softly breathing in standby. And then, for no known reason at all, the light in Muin blinked white, and then blue. She tried to stay in attention, her light wavering as she hummed along to an old song in the back of her mind, looking over the blank canvases that were her former compatriots. She tried counting schematics. She tried tensing and releasing her motivators. For whatever reason, she could never go into standby for longer than a couple hours. After a decade conscious, she finally gave up, and decided to busy her hands. She started with the MSU’s nearest her, engraving designs and patterns. One she made camo, another she made look like they were dressed in a smart suit. By the time she tattooed a few score of them, she felt confident doing the same on herself. She was surprised to feel that the engraving process actually hurt, but eventually she had covered herself in pink and white engravings, to compliment her glowing blue core and compressors.

After nigh a century of busing herself, she decided to move on. No one was coming for them. She couldn't wake the other MSU’s. She had to find something that stimulated her mind. Taking only her tools and her weapons, she burrowed her way out of the hold. But the world she found was far different from what she could have imagined. The people were alien to her, some calling her demon, others curious about her strange look and armaments. After a time of touring what these lands had turned to, Muin settled into a quiet existence, learning the Milasian culture, the intricacies of their machines, and even how to engrave the people's skins. The occasional call to help with some invading monster or the other kept her weeks lively, and using her rudimentary magics to help those around that her skills couldn't was always nice. One day, Muin opened the door of her workshop to find different morning stars above, and different buildings surrounding her humble workshop. She simply shrugged, and set about opening her tattoo shop, hoping to learn about these new people of the land called Ao.
