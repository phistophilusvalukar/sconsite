Goka was unlike any place his father had ever known. Built around one of the few great openings through Qiang Tian, the Wall of Heaven, the city served as a gateway between Tian Xia and the lands beyond the mountains. Travelers, merchants, adventurers, nobles, gamblers, and warriors from across Golarion passed through its streets. To Valerian, Goka was simply home. His father, Veyr, had first arrived in Goka on business. A traveler at heart, he had spent much of his life moving from place to place, following trade routes and chasing the next horizon. He worshipped Desna, believing that life was meant to be experienced rather than merely endured. During his stay in Goka, he met Shindranni, a talented and beautiful drake racer who had grown up among the city's bustling streets and racing grounds. Their meeting quickly became something more. Where Veyr was a wanderer, Shindranni was deeply rooted in Goka. She knew its alleys, markets, temples, racing tracks, and fighting schools. She was fascinated by the sword and held a particular reverence for Setsuna Kuga, the legendary "Perfect Swordswoman" and founder of Minkai. From Shindranni, Valerian inherited a love of the blade and an appreciation for the discipline required to master it. From his father, he inherited something very different. Veyr taught Valerian that not every conflict needed to be won with a sword. Words could be sharper than steel, and a well-timed lie could accomplish what a hundred soldiers could not. To Veyr, the clever warrior was the one who convinced his opponent that they had already lost before the fighting even began. His father's devotion to Desna instilled in him a love of freedom, travel, and the unknown, while his mother's reverence for Tsukiyo and Shizuru taught him the value of reflection, honor, and the pursuit of perfection. As he grew older, Valerian found that none of these beliefs felt contradictory. He eventually came to follow the Path of the Heavens, seeing the divine not as a single path that demanded his obedience, but as a collection of ideals worth striving toward. Desna represented the freedom to wander. Tsukiyo represented contemplation and the quiet wisdom found in darkness. Shizuru represented the discipline and excellence he sought through the sword. Growing up in Goka also gave Valerian an unusual appreciation for the city's dragons, drakes, and kobolds. The city's kobold communities fascinated him, and he spent much of his youth listening to their stories of dragons, ancient treasures, and ancestral heroes. His mother's experience as a drake racer only strengthened that fascination. Valerian learned to recognize individual drakes by temperament and behavior. Eventually, he developed a particular fondness for them. As he grew older, his relationship with Goka's kobolds became more personal. He learned their customs, listened to their stories, and occasionally found himself dragged into their petty disputes. In return, they taught him things no formal instructor could: how to recognize a lie from a dragon's servant, how to read the movement of a frightened drake, and perhaps most importantly, how never to underestimate something simply because it was smaller than himself.

Valerian's martial training eventually brought him under the tutelage of the Butterfly Blades of Nai Yan Fei, the Empress of Goka. He learned that the best way to defeat an enemy was often to make them believe they had discovered the perfect opportunity to defeat you. And when they took the bait, Valerian struck. His skill with the sword grew alongside his talent for deception, eventually producing a fighting style that was as much performance as it was combat. As a personal challenge, he undertook the journey along Qiang Tian, the Wall of Heaven. The enormous mountain range seemed almost impossible to comprehend. To Valerian, that was precisely why he needed to walk it. He traveled along its paths alone, sometimes for days without speaking to another person. He slept beneath the stars, meditated in secluded places, and practiced his sword forms until his muscles ached. He meditated for hours, attempting to discover the inner reservoir of strength described by the martial traditions he had encountered. Sometimes he experienced strange visions. Once, while meditating beneath an open night sky, he swore he saw a second set of stars reflected beneath his closed eyes—stars that did not exist above him. Valerian's childhood had been spent watching the Ruby Phoenix Tournament. He remembered sitting beside his parents as a child, barely able to contain himself as legendary warriors entered the arena. He watched the crowds roar, the fighters clash, and the city's gamblers wager fortunes on warriors they had never met. He watched the drake races with the same fascination. Goka was a city where anything could become a spectacle, and Valerian loved it. As he grew older, the Ruby Phoenix Tournament became more than entertainment. It became an aspiration. One day, he wanted to stand among those warriors. And so, during the latest Ruby Phoenix Tournament, Valerian finally found himself there amongst the participants.

For all his preparation, nothing could have prepared Valerian for what happened next. The tournament was interrupted by the arrival of Mogaru, the Final King. The enormous kaiju descended upon Goka, turning celebration into chaos in moments. Buildings shook, crowds scattered, the roar of the creature drowned out everything else. Valerian stood frozen for only a moment. He reached for his sword; there were people in danger! His city was under attack!! He had spent his entire life training for moments like this. Valerian began to run toward the chaos. And then reality broke. There was no explosion. No spell. No warning. One moment he was standing in Goka. The next, the world around him rippled like a reflection disturbed by a stone. Valerian stumbled. The streets stretched impossibly far away; the sounds of Goka became distorted and distant. He caught glimpses of things that could not possibly be there—strange structures, unfamiliar skies, distant figures, and places that seemed to exist somewhere beyond the boundaries of the world. For a moment, Valerian saw Goka. Then he saw somewhere else. Then Goka again. His body felt as though it were being pulled in opposite directions. His armor grew unbearably heavy. His sword seemed impossibly distant. He could hear voices, though he could not understand them. Then came the pressure. An overwhelming force pressed against him from every direction, as though the universe itself were attempting to decide where he belonged. Valerian tried to fight it. Then everything went dark. When Valerian opened his eyes again, Goka was gone. He found himself lying beneath an unfamiliar sky, surrounded by a world that seemed to obey rules he did not understand. He slowly rose to his feet. His sword was still at his side. His armor was still intact. He was still himself. But something had changed. He could feel it. He had been pulled from Golarion and brought somewhere beyond anything his parents, his teachers, or even the dragons of Goka had ever described. It took time to learn the name of the place. Ao. And for the first time in his life, the wanderer who had always dreamed of seeing what lay beyond the horizon found himself somewhere that no traveler from Golarion had ever meant to reach.
