Now I commit this record to ink and parchment. For if I perish before I find my way back to my origin, my story, written by fate and played by me, will be remembered. Through it's will perhaps these words will make it back to my people. This is the tale of The Dark Empress. I was born under the embrace of the night sky in a small crux just north of Aelyosos. Being surrounded by the myth and legend of the land brought us closer to fate, and thus gave our settlement much more permanence than other cruxes. My guardians spoke much of the hero-gods of old and the cyclopean mysticism that gave them their pusedo-divinity. “This, too, was the will of fate” explained my father, who always took on the mask of a gravely old sage when teaching me history. Fate cast the Cyclops in the role of tragic enlightened seers–giving them an iota of it’s power to make their predictions, but dooming them to fall into darkness. A dramatic end. Naturally, after growing out of the phase of wanting to become a cyclops, I sought to understand every single facet of Fate. Starting with the ancient techniques of myth-speaking. This, too, was the will of fate. A century it took for my story to truly begin. In which Nocturne and I, my partner in everything, scoured the lands of Iblydos for all their secrets. The sins of the Ghol-Gan, the exact prophecies of hero-gods old and ancient–which always took the form of a story. When my knowledge proved sufficient I left the archipelago for my next task: The Harrow, and thus the Varisian people. Far north-west did I travel, taking on a mortal guise of a hooded masked figure cloaked head to toe, a course set for the lands of Ustalav. It was then, I met her.
12:48 PM
She had many names. Beauty incarnate, a wisp of the sun, fate’s gift to me. A simple woman in all honesty. She came to me at sunset while I posed as a street fortune-teller to unravel the secrets of The Harrow, all anxiety trapped behind scarlet eyes and fair skin. A divination she sought to reveal the fate behind an upcoming excursion, for she was a adventurer of little repute. Yes. The moment I laid eyes I knew she would be my muse, my hero-god. A crimson blaze for all of the world to witness. This, too, was the will of fate. She perished not more than a month after our first meeting. Her party overrun by the forces of evil, but they live now because she gave her life for theirs. I laid eyes upon her and she breathed her last. Surrounded by carnage and viscera. She could not have possibly recognized me without my guise, atop my steed cloaked in shadow, headless, but she smiled anyway when she saw me. She spoke highly of heroics and she got her wish. I cannot see the appeal. I thought I had grew complacent. Trying to play god-maker. So I threw myself into my work as a proper sibyl for my peers. Going from crux to crux teaching all that I had learned, while fulfilling my role as a proper Dullahan. Excising any who would disrupt fate, or escape their destiny. I cloaked my heart in shadows, finally becoming The Dark Empress. My mentors. They worried for my heart, that I had become the mask. They urged me to face whatever darkness clung to me, lest I forget myself and become a wretch. I knew they were right. After twenty-eight years I returned to her. It was pouring that night, with devastating winds. So much so that it seemed like the gods were weeping for her. It was easy to find her grave amongst the others–a blade shattered at the fuller lay on the soil facing her headstone. Rust beginning to eat at it. I dismounted Nocturne and approached. The moment my finger touched her head stone the world swam with color. Earth cracked under her grave and split apart, sending Nocturne and I falling towards the infinite. I lost consciousness for and inordinate amount of time, but I dreamed. I never dreamed before, but I dreamed of her. When I woke my surroundings were different. Everything felt strange. Then I heard her voice and looked down. Parthenas was in my lap, her scarlet eyes boring into where I assume my face would be if I had one. I could hear her voice again. I’m not sure if I will ever wake from this nightmare.
